# Robotics

Robots have a lot of cameras.
They also tend to be somewhere with a terrible network: cellular, satellite, mesh radio, or nothing at all.
And everybody wants to watch: the operator, the fleet dashboard, the guy in the back with an iPad.

Existing protocols make the robot pay for every viewer, and for every frame nobody is watching.

## Why MoQ

- **Pull, not push.** A robot only sends the tracks somebody subscribed to. Nobody watching camera 7? Camera 7 doesn't touch the uplink.
- **One uplink, any number of viewers.** The robot publishes once to a relay, and the relay fans out. Viewer #100 costs the robot nothing.
- **Latency over quality.** When the link degrades, MoQ skips old frames instead of buffering them. An operator wants what's happening *now*.
- **More than video.** Telemetry, control, and logs are just more tracks on the same connection and the same relays.
- **Watch in a browser.** Operators open a tab. No plugins, no WebRTC signaling servers, no VPN to the robot.

I wrote about the pull model in more detail in [On a Boat](/blog/on-a-boat).
